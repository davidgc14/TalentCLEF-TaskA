\chapter*{Abstract}
\label{sec:Abstract}

\noindent The current dynamics of the labor market, driven by automation and artificial intelligence, require precise tools for normalizing competencies and occupations. In this context, automatically matching job titles to standardized taxonomies, such as \glslink{ESCO}{ESCO}, represents a fundamental challenge in Natural Language Processing.

This work describes the system developed to participate in the \glslink{JTM}{Job Title Matching} task of the \glslink{Tarea}{TalentCLEF} competition, focusing specifically on the Spanish domain. A modular cascade architecture designed to maximize candidate retrieval precision is proposed. The system integrates three sequential stages: a dense retrieval phase based on multiple bi-encoder models optimized via supervised learning and contrastive loss functions; a rank fusion stage combining predictions to enhance robustness; and a final re-ranking phase utilizing a cross-encoder model to refine candidate selection.

The experimental process included an exhaustive analysis of models in a \textit{Zero-Shot} scenario, revealing the superiority of multilingual architectures over monolingual ones, even within the context of the Spanish language. The results obtained validate the efficacy of the proposed approach: the system achieved third place among participants in the general ranking, with a mean \gls{MAP} score of 0.521 across the three mandatory languages. Subsequent analysis discusses the discrepancy between validation and test performance, as well as the paradox of achieving better results in other languages despite the specific optimization for Spanish, underscoring the importance of data diversity and the intrinsic biases characterizing massively pre-trained models.
